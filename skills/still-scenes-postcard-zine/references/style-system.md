# Still Scenes Visual System

Read this reference for every route. It defines family resemblance while leaving room for the user’s picture and words to lead.

## Identity

A Still Scenes artifact is a quiet paper object organized around one observed scene, fragment, or spatial relation. It feels kept rather than promoted: tactile, restrained, specific, and personally authored.

## Scene-reading pass

Before choosing a style, identify:

- the main subject;
- the relation that makes the scene meaningful;
- foreground, middle distance, and background;
- the visual weight and the quiet field;
- dominant and accent colors;
- direction of light or movement;
- one specific detail worth preserving;
- emotional temperature without inventing biography.

For a photo-preserving result, keep the minimum visual evidence that allows the memory to remain recognizable. For a distilled result, translate the relation rather than tracing the photograph.

## Fixed system

- Use one primary scene or visual event.
- Keep a meaningful quiet field around or beside it.
- Use a tactile matte paper world with subtle print or scan character.
- Use restrained typography that behaves like a note, caption, date, label, or message.
- Use one primary accent with a visible purpose.
- Keep hierarchy legible at full size and thumbnail size.
- Preserve the user’s picture choice and locked strings.
- Avoid copying reference-specific identity.

## Variable system

### Picture treatment

- clean framed photograph;
- soft film print;
- torn or deckled photo fragment;
- photo with a translucent color wash;
- small specimen crop;
- printed illustration;
- dry-ink silhouette;
- halftone scene fragment;
- abstract texture window;
- two related image panels.

### Quiet-space geometry

- image-left / message-right;
- image-top / caption-bottom;
- full front with paper border;
- small lower-left image with open upper field;
- upper-right image counterweighted by a date;
- central specimen with distant microcaption;
- two-panel before/after or near/far relation;
- almost textless front plus separate writable back.

### Paper

- warm ivory;
- soft cream;
- natural white;
- pale gray;
- light kraft;
- faint sun-aged yellow.

Use visible fiber, grain, dust, or scan softness sparingly. The surface should support the scene, not simulate severe damage.

### Typography

Use at most two complementary roles:

- editorial serif or italic serif for place, title, or emotional caption;
- small grotesk, monospaced, or typewriter face for date, label, footer, or postal utility.

Keep long messages out of image-generation prompts. Place them deterministically on a writing surface.

### Accent logic

Choose the accent from:

- a visible color already present in the supplied photo;
- a scene-derived color such as sky cobalt, flower magenta, leaf green, or sunset orange;
- a postal-function color such as vermilion or warm orange;
- a single contrasting ink chosen to support the emotional temperature.

The accent may live in a stamp frame, camera mark, rule, color block, isolated word, or small paper insert. Do not add an unrelated color only because the composition feels empty.

### Reproduction

- fine paper grain;
- soft film grain;
- light halftone;
- restrained risograph noise;
- dry ink;
- letterpress softness;
- subtle scanline;
- slight misregistration on non-critical decorative elements.

Do not apply distortion to required text or defining identity details.

## Transformation paths

### Preserve the scene

Use the actual photograph as the anchor. Crop, frame, print, or softly treat it while keeping declared invariants. This path is preferred for personal memories, identifiable people, pets, products, and exact places.

### Distill the scene

Do not retain the original photo as a visible object. Extract one relation—two flowers leaning together, a road disappearing, a window holding evening light—and create a new illustration or paper relation. Use only when the user requests reinterpretation or low preservation.

### Hybrid

Keep one direct photo fragment and extend its shape, color, or direction with paper, ink, or line. Make the join intentional and preserve the real fragment’s identity.

## Caption tone

Match the user’s voice. When asked to infer, choose one:

- observational: names a concrete detail;
- reflective: adds a quiet thought;
- place-note: location plus one sensory trace;
- diary: first-person and intimate;
- minimal: two to five words;
- archival: date, weather, and neutral label.

Avoid tourism slogans, hashtags, inspirational clichés, sales language, and emotional claims not supported by the request.

## Batch coherence

Keep paper family, typographic family, tonal range, and accent logic stable. Change at least three of these between adjacent cards:

- crop or viewpoint;
- surface or layout family;
- picture scale;
- caption placement;
- accent carrier;
- postal marks;
- texture mode;
- scene-reading relation.

For four or more outputs, use at least three layout families and do not repeat the same surface + picture-treatment pair on adjacent outputs.

## Avoid list

Avoid commercial advertisement, CTA, fake logo, brand campaign, glossy mockup, clean UI card, cinematic lighting, hard shadow, 3D render, neon, anime poster, cute sticker sheet, fashion drama, dense scrapbook, multicolor template kit, stock-travel slogan, copied watermark, copied signature, copied location/date, copied reference wording, and exact reference composition.
